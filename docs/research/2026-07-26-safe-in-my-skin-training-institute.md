# Safe In My Skin Training Institute practice-source review

Reviewed: 2026-07-26

Safe In My Skin Training Institute (SIMTI) is useful to JeloCare as a Nigerian
practice and education signal. It is not, by itself, a clinical-evidence source
or a product-approval authority.

## Why it matters

SIMTI describes a pharmacy-focused move from “guess or sell” to “assess and
advise.” That distinction matches JeloCare’s north star: help a person
understand what may fit, explain the boundary, and find a credible place to buy
without becoming a store or a diagnostic service.

Its public teaching currently highlights several locally relevant questions:

- how pharmacists distinguish a damaged barrier, self-described sensitive skin,
  and a possible irritant reaction before recommending a product;
- how irritation and post-inflammatory dark marks can interact on deeper skin
  tones;
- how to keep children’s routines simple and avoid adult-strength trend
  products;
- how common Nigerian self-treatment language, including “disinfecting” acne or
  asking for an antibiotic, should trigger education and referral rather than a
  sale;
- how AI can support a structured consultation without replacing a qualified
  professional.

These are strong discovery inputs for JeloCare’s concern ontology, vocabulary,
question order, referral boundaries, and pharmacist-facing workflows.

## Evidence boundary

Treat a SIMTI post, lesson, case study, or curriculum statement as a
`practice-signal`:

1. It may identify a real question, misconception, phrase, workflow gap, or
   teaching opportunity in Nigerian practice.
2. It may shape the research question and the language used to ask it.
3. It must not establish a diagnosis, contraindication, efficacy claim,
   ingredient rule, paediatric rule, or product recommendation on its own.
4. Every public health or clinical conclusion must still be checked against
   current guidance from an official health system, public-health agency,
   professional society, regulator, or appropriate peer-reviewed primary
   literature.
5. Named products in a lesson remain examples. They do not receive catalogue,
   care, ranking, or retailer approval from that mention.

This keeps local expertise visible without flattening a school’s teaching,
manufacturer copy, retailer copy, community experience, and clinical guidance
into one unsafe source tier.

## How agents should use the source

For each useful SIMTI item:

1. Preserve the exact public URL, publication date, author or presenter, and
   access date.
2. Extract the user question or decision boundary, not a stronger conclusion.
3. Search for the authoritative clinical sources that support, narrow, or
   contradict it.
4. Write JeloCare copy in observable, plain language. Do not expose diagnostic,
   biochemical, or backend terminology merely because the source uses it.
5. Keep products suppressed whenever examination, pharmacist review, or urgent
   care is the safer next step.
6. Record the final sources and review date in the relevant concern, ingredient,
   or Ask Jelo record.

An unverified practice signal belongs in the research backlog, not in public
guidance.

## First research queue

## Partnership due diligence

Do not add a trust badge, accreditation claim, curriculum endorsement, or
clinical-review label yet. Before a formal relationship, confirm:

- the current curriculum outline and learning outcomes;
- educator and reviewer credentials relevant to each module;
- the bibliography and update process behind clinical teaching;
- assessment, supervision, conflict-of-interest, and product-sponsorship rules;
- how learner cases and patient information are consented, de-identified, and
  retained;
- whether any claimed accreditation or continuing-professional-development
  status can be verified with the issuing body.

JeloCare may later invite SIMTI to review Nigerian consultation language or
pharmacist workflows. Clinical facts and product records must continue through
JeloCare’s normal evidence and publication gates.
